# Writing bullets that get read

A recruiter spends 5–20 seconds on a first pass. The bullet is the unit they
read, so the bullet is the unit that has to work.

## The shape

Every bullet: **action verb → what you did → context → impact.**

The XYZ form: *Accomplished [X], measured by [Y], by doing [Z].*

> Built a customer billing pipeline processing 2M invoices/month, cutting
> reconciliation errors from 3% to 0.1%

Note what that does: the reader learns the scope (2M/month), the result (3% →
0.1%), and the mechanism (built it). Compare:

> Responsible for billing pipeline improvements

Same job. The second one tells the reader nothing, and "responsible for" is a
phrase that actively signals you have nothing to say.

## Domain context is the differentiator

This is the single highest-leverage thing most resumes leave out.

> Developed web applications

> Developed fintech web applications for credit management, card issuance, and
> transaction processing, under PCI scope, serving 40k B2C customers

Both are true. The second one tells a fintech recruiter "this person has worked
in my world." Always ask yourself: what industry, what system, what data
sensitivity, who were the users.

## Verbs

Strong, past tense, specific. Openers that work:

Led · Built · Designed · Engineered · Architected · Implemented · Shipped ·
Launched · Drove · Reduced · Cut · Grew · Scaled · Migrated · Automated ·
Mentored · Coached · Owned · Rebuilt · Eliminated · Accelerated ·
Consolidated · Standardized · Negotiated · Authored · Refactored · Benchmarked

Verbs that waste a line:

Responsible for · Worked on · Helped · Assisted · Participated · Duties included ·
Tasked with · Contributed to

Inflated words that add length without information — cut them:

Spearheaded · Orchestrated · Utilized · Leveraged · Synergized · Pioneered ·
Best-in-class · World-class · Dynamic · Results-driven · Detail-oriented ·
Proven track record

Adjectives cannot be verified. Numbers can.

> Excellent communication skills

> Presented quarterly roadmap to the C-suite across 3 business units

## Numbers

Quantify wherever the reader can verify it. Use digits: `8`, not `eight`.

Good sources of numbers: volume (users, requests, records), time (saved,
reduced), money (revenue, cost, budget), percentage change, team size, uptime,
error rates, latency.

**When you do not know the number, do not invent it.** Write
`[confirm: what metric]` in your profile so it becomes a question you answer,
not a claim you ship. A fabricated metric is discoverable in an interview, and
it is falsification.

If the work was pre-launch or internal with no usage figure, scale something else:
migration count, module count, team size, coverage, test count.

## Bullets are not sentences

- No trailing period. Fragments read as deliberate; sentences read as prose.
- No personal pronouns. `I led a team` → `Led a team`. The implied subject is you.
- Keep them under two lines in the final layout. A bullet that wraps to three
  lines is a bullet that will not be read.

## Order bullets by relevance to the posting

Reverse-chronological holds across roles, but within a role, put the two or three
bullets that map to the posting's top requirements first. That is the tailoring,
and it is legitimate: you are choosing what to lead with, not claiming what you
did not do.

## The bad-to-good set

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Responsible for developing web applications | Built fintech web apps for card issuance and payment processing under PCI scope |
| Improved performance | Cut p99 API latency 840ms → 210ms via Redis caching and query tuning |
| Worked with the data team | Designed a PostgreSQL schema serving 30 analysts, removing 2TB of duplication |
| Helped deploy services | Shipped 9 services to 3 AWS regions behind GitHub Actions CI/CD |
| Strong problem solver | Resolved a cascading replication failure that had stalled the release train |
